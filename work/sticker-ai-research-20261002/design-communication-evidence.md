# Evidence on sticker design and conversational meaning

Research date and retrieval date for every source below: 2026-10-02.

This is a bounded evidence report for the Primary's integration decision. It is not an accepted design or a final SOP. No sticker was generated, no user test was run, and no third-party visual asset was downloaded or imported.

## Decision supported by the evidence

A sticker brief needs a conversational purpose in addition to an emotion. The evidence supports distinguishing what the character depicts, what the sender intends to do, and what the recipient understands. A pleasing picture or a correctly recognized emotion does not establish that a sticker makes an appropriate reply.

The strongest directly relevant evidence combines a study of actual recalled sticker uses, a controlled sticker emotion-recognition task, and professional instruction on pose design. These sources answer different questions. Neither animation teaching nor emotion recognition proves successful communication in a real chat.

## Primary source register

All entries provide direct source URLs. Dates are publication dates unless marked otherwise. Evidence summaries are paraphrases. Dates reported by search-engine crawl metadata were not treated as publication dates.

### D1. Everyday usefulness and visibility

- Source: LINE Creators Market, [Creation guidelines: Stickers](https://creator.line.me/en/guideline/sticker/).
- Author and date: LINE; no publication date visible.
- Evidence kind: Official platform creator guidance; HTML read.
- Finding: LINE recommends understandable expressions, messages, and illustrations that work in daily conversation. It discourages poor visibility, excessively elongated/full-length tall character compositions, and packs with little variety.
- Limit: These are LINE recommendations, not experimentally established universal rules. Platform export dimensions belong to a separate research branch.

### D2. Caption treatment belongs in the chat preview

- Source: LINE Creators Market, [Creating message stickers](https://creator.line.me/en/guideline/messagesticker/detail/).
- Author and date: LINE; no publication date visible.
- Evidence kind: Official guidance for a particular editable-caption sticker product; source text read.
- Finding: LINE provides chat preview and instructs creators to check the result there. It recommends text outlines to preserve visibility over users' backgrounds and encourages varied text placements.
- Limit: The product's editable captions and supported text directions are not universal sticker capabilities. The page does not establish that a caption always improves comprehension.

### D3. Meaning must lead the pose

- Source: [Tutorial: Building Appealing Character Poses for Animation](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/tutorial-building-appealing-character-poses-for-animation/).
- Author and date: Animation Mentor, teaching by Anthony Wong; 2026-04-07.
- Evidence kind: Primary professional teaching; article text read, embedded video not watched.
- Finding: Wong evaluates meaning, silhouette, line of action, and counter-pose. He explains that an important gesture can become unreadable when buried inside the body silhouette, and that posing needs a coherent action and credible balance.
- Limit: Examples concern animation rigs and mainly humanlike anatomy. Applying their mechanics to tentacles requires adaptation, not literal copying of hands, knees, or shoulders.

### D4. Acting can use the body and its interaction with objects

- Source: [Tutorial: Animating Emotion Through Body Language](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/tutorial-animating-emotion-through-body-language/).
- Author and date: Animation Mentor, teaching by Natasha Krinsky; 2026-06-30.
- Evidence kind: Primary professional teaching; article text read, embedded video not watched.
- Finding: The lesson emphasizes posture, overall silhouette, weight, timing, and rhythm on rigs with limited facial acting. Its discussed examples include clothing and phones participating in emotional poses. The body components should support the same acting idea.
- Limit: Limited facial controls do not mean a completely faceless subject. This supports testing body-led expression, not promising universal recognition of a particular faceless mascot.

### D5. Deformation is constrained by the character and material

- Source: [Squash and Stretch: The 12 Basic Principles of Animation](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/squash-and-stretch-the-12-basic-principles-of-animation/).
- Author and date: Chris Hurtt, Animation Mentor; 2017-06-05.
- Evidence kind: Primary animation teaching; article text read.
- Finding: Squash and stretch can animate an entire object or body part, including the classic flour-sack exercise. The amount suggests material properties. Hurtt warns against losing the model or believability and discusses preserving perceived volume, with deliberate stylistic exceptions.
- Limit: This is craft guidance, not a measured identity-preservation threshold. It does not authorize changing DreamWeaver's anatomy or imply that maximum exaggeration is best.

### E1. Sticker use differs from an emotion-label inventory

- Source: [Sticker and Emoji Use in Facebook Messenger: Implications for Graphicon Change](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/d133da65-199f-4ed2-a061-4b9e2eddc5ef/content).
- Authors and date: Artie Konrad, Susan C. Herring, David Choi; 2020, Journal of Computer-Mediated Communication 25, 217-235.
- Evidence kind: Original peer-reviewed research; university-hosted full PDF text read, especially methods and findings.
- Sample and method: 13 interviews with active Messenger users near Menlo Park; subsequent 2,275-response English-language survey, comprising 872 sticker senders and 1,403 emoji senders. Data collection was in 2014. Respondents recalled their most recent sent item.
- Finding: Shared functions include acknowledgment, tone adjustment, and reciprocal exchange. Sticker preferences particularly included specific standalone meanings, opening/closing conversation, personality expression, humor, and playful interaction. Stickers were sent to closer recipients on average.
- Limit: Self-report and deliberately separated recent sticker/emoji-user groups; neither a controlled comprehension test nor current Chinese-platform usage. Historical product constraints cannot be generalized to all platforms today.

### E2. A sticker can soften an awkward exchange

- Source: [On Stickers and Communicative Fluidity in Social Media](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/2056305115578137).
- Author and date: Sun Sun Lim; 2015, Social Media + Society; DOI 10.1177/2056305115578137.
- Evidence kind: Original reflective essay grounded in the author's LINE exchanges; full PDF text read.
- Observation in the source: During a travel-service exchange, stickers participated in apologizing, showing alignment, appreciation, and handling awkward requests or payment discussions.
- Limit: One narrated interaction involving three people, not a controlled experiment or generalizable success rate. Social smoothing is not automatically the technical conversation-analysis category of repair.

### E3. Sticker emotion interpretation has been tested directly

- Source: [Gleaning Emotions from Virtual Stickers: An Intercultural Study](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/27523543231188778?download=true).
- Authors and date: Dongdong Yang, David J. Atkin, Laura Labato; first online 2023-08-30, Emerging Media 1(1), 110-130.
- Evidence kind: Original peer-reviewed survey with an interpretation task; publisher PDF methods/results/limitations read.
- Sample and task: 276 college students began; 193 usable responses after eligibility and attention checks. Participants were US-born lifelong US residents from one university, average age 19.10. They selected one of six emotions for each of 36 Chinese stickers, half animated and half static. Selection relied on facial-action criteria.
- Finding: Sticker-use frequency, empathy, and intercultural communication competence were associated with recognition accuracy. The reported mean accuracy was 0.86.
- Limit: A forced-choice, deliberately explicit-emotion stimulus set with potential ceiling effects, no conversational turn context, and no faceless-mascot test. The result is neither a universal comprehension guarantee nor a target acceptance score for this pack.

### E4. Emoji ambiguity is related evidence, not sticker equivalence

- Source: [Blissfully Happy or Ready to Fight: Varying Interpretations of Emoji](https://grouplens.org/site-content/uploads/Emoji_Interpretation_Paper.pdf).
- Authors and date: Hannah Miller, Jacob Thebault-Spieker, Shuo Chang, Isaac Johnson, Loren Terveen, Brent Hecht; ICWSM 2016.
- Evidence kind: Original empirical paper; author research-group PDF read.
- Sample and task: 304 retained US Mechanical Turk participants produced 4,560 interpretations. The study ultimately analyzed 22 emoji across five platform renderings. Questions elicited meaning, sentiment, and situations of use.
- Finding: Interpretation disagreement occurred even for the same rendering; different platform renderings added ambiguity.
- Limit: Isolated emoji, old renderings, US online participants. The study offers a useful open-response testing pattern, not a measured failure rate for rich stickers or current DreamWeaver designs.

### E5. Shared meaning can be tested through another person's selection

- Source: [Small Stickers, Big Meanings: A Multilingual Sticker Semantic Understanding Dataset with a Gamified Approach](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.01668v1).
- Authors and date: Heng Er Metilda Chee, Jiayin Wang, Zhiqiang Guo, Weizhi Ma, Min Zhang; arXiv v1, 2025-06-02. The inspected version says under review.
- Evidence kind: Original preprint and annotation study; full HTML sections on game design and sampling read.
- Method: Labelers supply plausible search queries; different players retrieve stickers from candidates. The project reports over 60 contributors and about 60 hours. It permits skips and manually reviews unsuccessful matches.
- Finding: The authors observed that failed target retrieval can reflect several semantically suitable stickers, not necessarily a bad interpretation.
- Limit: Query matching is not real recipient response. Selection filters excluded short-reply-heavy conversations. Counts differ slightly between abstract and tables, so exact dataset totals should not be imported uncritically.

### E6. Emotion and intention are separate model inputs

- Source: [Emotion and Intention Guided Multi-Modal Learning for Sticker Response Selection](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/38509).
- Authors and date: Yuxuan Hu, Jian Chen, Yuhao Wang, Zixuan Li, Jing Xiong, Pengyue Jia, Wei Wang, Chengming Li, Xiangyu Zhao; 2026-03-14, AAAI 40(17), 14883-14891.
- Evidence kind: Original proceedings landing page and abstract only.
- Finding claimed by authors: Modeling emotion and intention separately can produce contextual mismatches; their joint model improves selection on two public datasets.
- Limit: Full experimental protocol and effect sizes were not inspected. This is a useful conceptual lead, not human-user validation or evidence that a particular pack communicates correctly.

### E7. Synthetic conversations need a different evidence label

- Source: [STICKERCONV: Generating Multimodal Empathetic Responses from Scratch](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.417/).
- Authors and date: Yiqun Zhang and coauthors; ACL 2024.
- Evidence kind: Original ACL Anthology abstract read.
- Finding: The described dataset-building approach uses collaborating agents to simulate conversations with stickers.
- Limit: Synthetic conversational plausibility is not evidence of observed human use. This report does not import its benchmark scores or assume its labels validate a designed sticker pack.

## Communicative distinctions relevant to pack planning

The following is a local design interpretation of E1-E3, not a universal or exhaustive taxonomy. One sticker can serve several purposes, depending on the preceding turn and relationship.

| Intended conversational job | Why an emotion label is insufficient | Candidate test prompt |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acknowledge receipt | A recipient can understand without agreeing or celebrating. | After a practical instruction, does this mean received, yes, or thanks? |
| Request or response | Wanting attention, agreeing, refusing, and asking someone to wait can share a similar mood. | What action does the sender want next? |
| Relationship tone | Gratitude, comfort, affection, and deference concern the other person. | Who would you send this to, and who would make it feel inappropriate? |
| Humor or stance | Sarcasm, teasing, and self-mockery may differ from the character's depicted feeling. | Who is the joke about? Is the sender sincere? |
| Open, sustain, or close | A greeting, playful exchange, and gentle goodbye change conversation flow. | Would you reply, continue the joke, or stop chatting? |
| Clarify or soften trouble | Confusion, apology, reassurance, and a request to repeat address different problems. | What misunderstanding or awkwardness would this resolve? |

The final row needs particular care. E2 supports social smoothing, but this branch did not verify an original sticker-specific study that measures successful repair of misunderstandings. Treat repair designs as a local hypothesis and validate the intended next turn.

## Application to a faceless DreamWeaver

The following anatomy comes from the assignment brief, not from an image independently inspected in this branch: purple fluffy faceless body; four tentacles connected to a blue dream-weaving net; gold star in the dark front; gold-ball antennae. The dark front and star are not a human face. The exact permitted movement of antennae, star, net, and their attachment points remains a design question for the Primary and the existing character reference.

Local inference from D3-D5: available acting channels include body tilt and compression, the direction and openness of tentacle arrangements, antenna orientation if flexible, and how the net is held or tensioned. Net interaction can give an action a concrete object and consequence. None of these channels has a fixed meaning independent of context.

Candidate contrasts for a bounded prototype, not approved poses or recipes:

| Candidate intent | Mechanism worth testing | Plausible misreading to test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Received / understood | A restrained orientation change and a settled, controlled hold on the net | Bowing, thanking, or looking down |
| Please wait / working on it | Tentacles actively engaged with a partly worked net; body oriented toward the task | Ignoring the recipient or being tangled |
| Confused / please explain | Uneven body lean and interrupted net action, while all four tentacles remain accounted for | Broken anatomy, distress, or physical imbalance |
| Thank you / appreciation | Presenting or carefully cradling a net-held result | Offering a gift, requesting one, or claiming ownership |
| Too much / overwhelmed | Body compressed under a visibly troublesome net interaction | Sadness, exhaustion, or injury |

These candidates deliberately expose uncertainty. They are not evidence that those poses work. A short caption may anchor a required speech act, but captioned and uncaptioned versions should be judged separately. Emotional decoration must not conceal missing mechanics.

Local recommendation: hold identity and construction stable while exploring pose. Account for every tentacle and connection even where occlusion hides one. Preserve the source's body, materials, palette, star, and antenna design unless a variation is explicitly selected. Treat deformation as a controlled range chosen from the actual reference, not a license to invent arms, fingers, eyes, eyebrows, or a mouth. A body-led pose can change while a model remains recognizable; a locked neutral silhouette for every item would prevent that exploration.

## Testing recommendations for the Primary to select

These are local proposed tests informed by the sources, not a validated universal protocol. They require target recipients; AI-only review can flag defects but cannot establish human comprehension.

### Per item

1. Ask for an unprompted paraphrase before showing the intended label. Collect what the recipient thinks the sender means, not just the character's visible mood.
2. Place the item after a plausible previous message. Ask what reply or action the recipient expects next. Include a second context where the same image could turn sarcastic, dismissive, or intimate.
3. Compare neighboring intents, such as received versus agreed, thanks versus please, tired versus sad, or celebration versus mockery. Let reviewers select multiple acceptable meanings or none.
4. Inspect the item at its actual chat display size and in its picker thumbnail. Check whether the decisive pose, net interaction, and identity marks survive reduction. Check relevant light and dark chat backgrounds.
5. If a caption is used, evaluate the whole captioned item. A separate caption-hidden check can diagnose what the image contributes; it must not become an arbitrary ban on text-led stickers. Check line breaks, clipping, contrast, and whether image and wording contradict each other.
6. Record the intended meaning, observed paraphrases, useful contexts, unwanted meanings, and the exact revision. Keep a mismatch visible instead of translating it into a subjective beauty score.

### Per pack

1. Give reviewers realistic chat turns and the whole pack. Observe which items they choose and when they prefer plain text or no sticker. This tests selection and missing functions.
2. Compare items chosen for the same turns. Remove accidental duplicates or distinguish their tone, intensity, relationship, or expected reply. Several valid choices are acceptable when the difference is intentional.
3. Ask target users for occasions when they would actually send each item. If an attractive item has no plausible use, investigate whether it belongs in the pack.
4. Check identity and anatomical consistency across the contact sheet, then return to individual-size previews. Pack uniformity should not erase useful pose variation.
5. Where the audience crosses languages, ages, or communities, collect interpretations from those groups separately. Do not infer culture from a single reviewer or translate a joke word for word and assume equivalent tone.
6. Report who participated, which version and contexts they saw, observed confusions, and untested audiences. Set any acceptance threshold before a formal test and justify it locally. No universal reaction count, font size, margin, or score is supplied by this report.

## Visual inspection and unresolved evidence

No public representative artwork was actually visually inspected in this branch. Calls requesting screenshots of the official [LINE Sticker Expression Guide](https://lineforbusiness.com/files/en_LINE%20Sticker%20Expression%20Guide_2018.pdf), PDF pages 6 and 9, returned source references without image content visible to this agent. Those calls therefore do not count as visual inspection. Text or alt-text summaries were not converted into observations about shape, style, or successful communication. The guide was not used to establish current platform rules.

The original [A pragmatic typology of WhatsApp sticker functions](https://www.benjamins.com/online/prag/articles/prag.24063.lin), Esther Linares Bernabeu and Francisco Yus, online 2025-12-15, surfaced as a relevant contemporary lead. Publisher search extracts describe an intention/communication/interpretation distinction, but direct article and print opens produced tool internal errors. Full methods were not verified, so corpus percentages and sample details are intentionally not adopted here. No login, denial, or rate-limit challenge was bypassed.

Further unresolved questions are whether this exact mascot remains recognizable under strong deformation, which of its parts may move, whether its net can temporarily change shape or carry objects, and which recipients can distinguish its proposed replies without text. These require the actual character reference and designed examples. Current sources do not settle them.

## Prioritized local recommendations

1. Define each candidate through previous message, sender purpose, recipient interpretation, relationship, and unwanted reading before choosing a visual recipe.
2. Establish DreamWeaver's anatomical and semantic invariants from its real reference before any style extraction. Do not retrofit human facial reactions onto the dark front.
3. Explore body and net acting through a small set of deliberately different intents; inspect real rendered results and revise the ambiguous ones before expanding a pack.
4. Make chat-context selection and recipient paraphrase part of acceptance. Keep beauty, character identity, emotion recognition, and conversational usefulness as separate findings.
5. Label references by evidence type. Craft guidance supports design choices; user studies support bounded claims; synthetic model benchmarks do not replace human use tests.

Evidence scope: 12 primary sources used, consisting of 5 official creator/animation-teaching sources, 5 original academic full-text sources, and 2 original model-paper abstracts. Two additional leads are documented as gaps. Zero verified visual examples, zero designed sticker tests, and zero real-chat acceptance tests were completed in this branch.
