# Space conception (空间构想)

Read the [shared interaction contract](../oneirloom/references/interaction-contract.md) before the first substantive response unless its unchanged content is already available. For prompts with people, including people used to show scale, read [person defaults and proportions](../oneirloom/references/person-prompts.md).

Use this method to organize a depicted place. It resolves what occupies the space and how its parts relate before a camera or photographic treatment is chosen. A visual plan or prompt does not establish a buildable floor plan or engineering safety.

## Organize the place

1. Record the place's purpose, known dimensions or scale clues, fixed objects, and whether it is real or fictional.
2. Place the main masses and define what encloses the space. State where openings occur and what each opening connects or reveals.
3. Map adjacency and a continuous visible circulation route. Establish the focal area, scale relationships, and front, middle, and rear depth layers.
4. Read [spatial relations](references/spatial-relations.md) for the mass, enclosure, and circulation checks. Only after those relations hold, use [camera composition](../oneirloom-camera-composition/SKILL.md) to choose viewpoint and projection. [Photography](../oneirloom-style-photography/SKILL.md) owns photographic treatment. [Color and light](../oneirloom-color-light/SKILL.md) owns light and material response. Graphic hierarchy belongs to [graphic design](../oneirloom-style-design/SKILL.md).

Use relative scale when dimensions are unknown. Keep a visual concept distinct from a measured plan or construction claim.

## Correct a spatial miss

If a scene has no usable route through it, revise the openings and adjacency to show one continuous path before changing the camera. Keep the selected masses and focal area fixed unless they cause the blockage.

## Deliver the requested result

For a prompt request, integrate the resolved spatial relations into a complete prompt under the shared contract. For a spatial brief, describe masses, boundaries, openings, adjacency, circulation, focus, scale, and depth. State which dimensions or construction details remain unknown.
