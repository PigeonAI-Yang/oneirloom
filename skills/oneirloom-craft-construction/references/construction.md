# Construction reference

Use this reference to describe how visible parts form a craft object. Keep a template's layout, palette, and presentation defaults inside that template. Construction language describes an image or concept; it is not proof that a piece can be built.

## Check the assembly

- **Unit and scale.** Name the repeated piece or sheet, its cross-section, and its size relative to recognizable details. Use a counted grid only when the requested deliverable and available evidence support one.
- **Arrangement and joins.** State the shared plane, row, spacing, overlap, fold, seam, or attachment that relates the units. Identify where separate parts touch or remain apart.
- **Thickness and edges.** Say how many layers are visible, which way a unit stands, and where a cut or stepped edge exposes thickness.
- **Support and contact.** Show what holds an overhang or layer, where the object touches a backing or surface, and whether the support is part of the visible design.
- **Irregularity and scale.** Keep hand variation local and compatible with the assembly. Relate small units to the full piece and the surrounding scene.
- **Presentation.** Choose a display view for the finished object only after its construction is clear. A flat subject motif can retain its own angle while the physical sheet is shown from above.

## Mechanisms

### Layered paper relief

- **ID:** `craft.layered-paper-relief`
- **Tags:** M, R
- **Owner:** `oneirloom-craft-construction`
- **Visible mechanism:** Cut paper layers have thickness. Their spacing, overlap, and exposed edges create occlusion and contact shadows. A backing or attachment supports each raised part.
- **Variables:** Layer count, cut contour, spacing, overlap order, paper colors, support points, and display angle.
- **Failure and correction:** If the result reads as a flat print, specify separate cut layers, visible side edges, a small depth gap, and shadows where one layer covers another. If a shape appears to float, identify its attachment or backing.
- **Source and evidence:** Independently authored from the local visual-capability plan. No external catalog text or image was imported. This entry is authored and unrendered; no generated result or repeatability is established.

### Fuse-bead sheet

- **ID:** `craft.fuse-bead-mosaic`
- **Tags:** R, M
- **Owner:** `oneirloom-craft-construction`
- **Visible mechanism:** Short cylindrical beads with central holes stand perpendicular to one shared plane. One solid bead color occupies each grid cell to encode the silhouette and major internal details. The piece is one bead thick and its outer edge steps along the grid.
- **Variables:** Bead size, grid scale, color palette, motif detail, optional border, backing, and display view. Treat optional recipe choices as local to the selected template.
- **Failure and correction:** If the beads lose their individual shape, state the central holes, rounded top rims, sidewalls, and one-bead-per-cell alignment. If a motif becomes hard to recognize, preserve its main silhouette and internal color relationships while simplifying small details.
- **Source and evidence:** Derived from the existing [fuse-bead template](../templates/fuse-bead/template.md) and its recorded source/result inspection. That evidence supports visible bead construction, not a counted pattern or physical buildability. This method entry has no new generated result.

## Worked decision: avatar beads (头像拼豆)

Keep the supplied avatar as the content source. Preserve its facing direction, outer silhouette, and a small set of identity-bearing features as the bead pattern. Describe one short, hole-centered cylinder in each grid cell, all standing in the same plane. If the deliverable shows a physical sheet, choose its near-overhead display view separately from the avatar's depicted angle. The visual method does not provide a verified bead count.
