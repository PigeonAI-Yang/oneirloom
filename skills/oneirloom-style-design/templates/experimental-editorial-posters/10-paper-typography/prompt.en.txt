Create a flat, standalone editorial poster in a portrait 2:3 composition, viewed straight on, with the paper surface filling the whole frame. Make the entire sheet warm off-white paper with a richly photographed, naturally crumpled texture: broad soft folds, fine creases, subtle grain, and shallow paper relief. Keep the folds visible across the design while preserving clear, readable print areas.

Set one oversized, elegant, high-contrast serif headline in near-black, tightly stacked at the upper left and left aligned. Let the broad, asymmetric headline block dominate roughly the upper two-thirds of the poster. Print the following four words on four separate lines, with the spelling, punctuation, and line breaks exactly as shown:

“Make”
“room”
“for”
“ideas.”

Draw one narrow, hand-marked warm-yellow oval around the word “room” only. Keep the letters unobscured inside the loop; make the oval a single slender stroke whose visible area is about five percent of the poster.

Place one small, tangible ball of crumpled matching paper below the headline toward the lower left. Give it a short, soft contact shadow tucked beneath its base so the paper ball rests believably on the sheet.

Place a tiny, neat, right-aligned uppercase note block on the right side, subordinate to the headline. Set its four lines exactly as follows:

“SAME”
“SPACE”
“NEW”
“THOUGHTS”

Set a tiny footer near the lower-left edge exactly as follows: “FIELD 10”. Keep the composition quiet and spacious, with the crisp serif headline as the main focal point, the marker loop as the sole bright accent, and every printed word legible across the photographed paper folds.
