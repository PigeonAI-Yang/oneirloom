Create one complete, standalone editorial poster on a portrait 2:3 canvas. Use panel 04 of the style reference for its torn-paper composition, printed texture, and grayscale palette with one yellow accent. The poster depicts one coherent coastal landscape of sea cliffs and breaking waves. Fill the canvas with warm off-white matte paper and a restrained, high-contrast flat-print collage.

Place the grayscale coast across roughly the upper half of the canvas and divide it into three horizontally torn image strips. Keep all three strips from the same coast, with recognizable continuity in the cliff contours, horizon, and wave foam. Give every strip its own slight displacement: move the top strip about 3% of the canvas width to the right and 1% of its height upward; move the middle strip about 2% of the canvas width to the left and 1% of its height downward; move the bottom strip about 1% of the canvas width to the right and 2% of its height downward. Make the offsets distinct but restrained, so the same coastline remains immediately recognizable after the tear. Show irregular fibrous white paper edges and narrow off-white gaps at each torn seam. Keep the cliffs, sea, horizon, and breaking foam clearly separated in grayscale tones.

Set the most prominent headline in the lower-middle area. Use heavy, tightly condensed black sans-serif lettering, left-aligned on two separate lines reading exactly “COAST” and “LINES”. Together the two lines occupy about 80% of the canvas width, with clear letterforms and tight spacing, forming a strong, stable text block beneath the coastal collage. Let the headline lead the visual hierarchy while the coastline and type read as one unified editorial poster.

Overlap one small, bright yellow torn-paper note at the right-side junction between the image and headline, crossing the lower-right edge of the coastal collage and the adjoining title area. Give the note subtle lifted paper edges and keep its area below 10% of the full canvas. Use clear black handwriting on exactly three lines with the exact words: “Edges” / “keep” / “changing”. Keep the note vivid but restrained and place its lettering clear of the headline letterforms.

Add the exact small, quiet black uppercase footer text “FIELD STUDIES” at the lower-left end of the bottom margin. Preserve the reading hierarchy: the tightly condensed headline is strongest, the grayscale coast supplies the scene, the yellow handwritten note is the third accent, and the footer is smallest. Keep the warm white paper, grayscale landscape, black type, and single yellow note in crisp contrast, with visible natural torn edges and a complete, balanced composition.
