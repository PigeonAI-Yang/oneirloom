Create a complete, standalone, front-facing vertical 2:3 editorial poster as a flat print on warm off-white paper with fine visible grain. A large black-and-white fox head in profile dominates the canvas and faces right; its pointed ears and tapered muzzle extending to the right are unmistakable. Build the fox as a strong dark silhouette in paper white and grayscale, with coarse photocopy grain, halftone flecks, and lightly broken ink along the edge and through the fur. Let the rough fur texture spread across the subject while keeping the full fox outline recognizable. Leave warm paper visible around the profile and a clean narrow type area on the right.

Across the lower-middle of the dark silhouette, draw a loose oversized two-line headline in purple marker, with thick strokes, slight changes in hand pressure, a few dry streaks, and a gentle upward slant from left to right. The first line must read exactly “STAY”; the second line must read exactly “CURIOUS”. Add one hand-drawn underline in the same purple just beneath “CURIOUS”, extending slightly to the right.

In the open right-side paper area, set a small, crisp black note as a vertical stack: first line exactly “SEE”, second line exactly “MORE”. At the lower-left paper margin, place a tiny but legible two-line black footer: first line exactly “FIELD 08”, second line exactly “A poster study”.

Make the fox silhouette and purple marker phrase the primary visual hierarchy, with the right note and footer as restrained microtype. Use black, white, gray, warm off-white paper, and purple as the single accent hue; purple appears only in the two handwritten headline lines and their underline. Keep all lettering accurately spelled and clearly separated in a concise, rough-textured photocopy zine poster.
