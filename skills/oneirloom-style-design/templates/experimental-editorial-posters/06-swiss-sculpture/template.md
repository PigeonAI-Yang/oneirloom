## Selection conditions

Choose this recipe when two outward-facing views and their different treatments support the message. Local rectangular fragments break the left-facing image and interleave with the main title. The right-facing image sits against a bounded accent field and can remain globally legible while a local rectangular interruption changes its middle tone. Smaller copy and footer sit lower-left. Image, text, and accent form one composition.

## Design the adaptation

Start from the current subject's understood form, connected parts, action, and meaning. Map the two views to the same identity with separately legible directions. Decide which half-body portions appear at left, which portions appear at right, and how crop and depth make each direction readable. Human profile cues do not transfer automatically to a nonhuman subject. A star or other emblem placed in a preset slot does not establish facing direction.

Retain the composition's contrast between local left fragmentation and a coherent right-facing body with a localized interruption. Keep fragment offsets near the subject rather than dividing the whole left paper field into bands. Resolve the vertical meeting edge separately from those cuts and from the right middle rectangle. Decide what remains recognizable through every treatment.

Recompose the left photographic pieces with continuous image-bearing coverage inside the intended body footprint. Shift pieces locally while their edges touch, overlap, or reveal the underlying photographic view. Let exterior paper follow deliberate silhouette steps. Do not insert white checkerboard cells between pieces. This coverage follows the actual anatomy and allows different underlying image content; it does not require a fixed contour or identical mirrored pixels.

Design the title and left fragments together. Specify which text crosses image, which fragments sit in front or behind it, and where direction cues remain visible. Keep the title's scale and reading order related to the body mass. Place supplied secondary copy and footer in the lower-left hierarchy. Omit unsupported text. Resolve the resulting space without inventing labels or moving the main title into an unrelated empty corner.

Use the accent field to support the right-facing body and maintain the intended contrast with paper. Resolve its bounds, border, and relation to the local darker rectangle. The rectangle is a deliberate local tonal or cut treatment, not a requirement to displace the whole right body. Do not erase it merely because the view is described as continuous.

Translate the reference's balance, crop, and negative space through the new anatomy. Its marble neck contour is not an anatomical mask for another subject. A broad creature may require different local contours to preserve recognizable body structure. Record such departures and judge their effect on the whole composition instead of maximizing coverage of an arbitrary skeleton.

For this creature example, the user confirms a purple, fluffy body with four tentacles and identifies its net or hammock as the action of weaving dreams. The illustration visibly shows a violet tufted body, dark oval panel, gold star and antenna tips, curled appendages, and blue strands suspending a crescent. Keep the user's identity description and confirmed meaning distinct from visible evidence and optional interpretations such as care or rest.

Carry the confirmed dream-weaving meaning through the half-body treatment. Preserve a legible connection between the creature and its net or hammock, or deliberately reinterpret that action while retaining its meaning. Do not discard the suspended assembly as an inconvenient accessory. Four tentacles define the full character; all four need not appear in each crop. Resolve visible anatomy without claiming to know hidden side details. Neither every-part visibility nor emblem-only recognition is a universal solution.

For the supplied title `DREAM WEAVER`, a two-word, two-line treatment differs from the source's taller four-line block. Resolve its scale, interleaving, and whitespace as a current design choice. Use the exact supplied `ONEIRLOOM` footer. Do not invent secondary copy. A hyperreal material treatment changes material, not the obligation to resolve identity, directions, and visual relationships. Choose grayscale, duotone, or retained identity color according to the brief.

Resolve the paper and printed finish alongside the composition. For this confirmed adaptation, use aged warm off-white rough uncoated paper with subtle visible fiber, tooth, and tonal irregularity. Give the printed image, black headline, and cobalt field a shared paper surface, with faint ink traces, restrained smudged or worn print edges, and uneven ink absorption. These describe the intended appearance, not a verified original printing process.

Keep the creature's fur convincingly photographic within that printed image. Apply restrained grain, edge wear, and tonal print variation to its final reproduction on the same sheet. Preserve readable letter silhouettes and the local dark subject segment. Generic vintage wording, an added grain instruction alone, random cracks or dirt, and exaggerated sepia stains do not specify this relationship. Deliver flat poster artwork, not a three-dimensional paper mockup.

Before execution, explain internally how the crop, two views, fragmentation, interwoven title, right accent, and middle interruption serve the message and character. Reinspect or redesign any relation that remains unresolved. Explicit user choices take precedence, including a requested change to view count, full-body coverage, or color. Label the adaptation when such a choice changes the source mechanism.

## Produce and hand off

Choose direct generation, native design, or compositing after the design is resolved. Honor the current deliverable, explicit tool or model constraints, text accuracy, and observed limitations. A sketch is optional and provisional. If used, include enough actual subject structure to judge direction and meaningful connections alongside the copy. Inspect the sketch's visual logic; do not lock its silhouette into a final anatomical mask.

For a whole-composition prompt, describe the two views, visible portions, local left strips interwoven with the title, lower-left secondary hierarchy, right accent, and localized dark interruption as one design. For a composite, retain those same decisions in the layout and prepare only the needed assets. An actual view may require a revised crop or outline. Do not force it into a marble-shaped placeholder. Mirroring is an adaptation choice, not proof of the original method, and must preserve required directional cues and product lettering.

Carry the join and finish decisions explicitly into either handoff. Prompt-ready wording for this adaptation is: "Recompose the locally shifted rectangular photographic pieces into a continuous image-bearing body. Their interior edges touch or overlap, with the underlying photographic view visible where a shifted piece uncovers it. Exterior warm paper follows the stepped silhouette. Print the convincing fur photograph, readable black headline, and cobalt field on one aged warm off-white rough uncoated sheet, with subtle fiber and tooth, uneven ink absorption, faint ink traces, and restrained worn print edges." Adapt this wording to the resolved anatomy and current copy while retaining the right dark rectangle and meaningful connections.

Requested artifact work returns the final preview and actual inspection status, plus editable source and assets when warranted. No generation or success claim follows from this recipe text alone.

## Review against the intended adaptation

- The subject remains recognizable and its intended action or meaning survives the crop and treatment. Important connected parts have a resolved role.
- The left-facing half-body and right-facing body read as the same identity with independent direction cues. An emblem placed on either side is insufficient evidence by itself.
- Local rectangular strips fragment the left body and interleave with the main title. Type hierarchy and overlap make a coherent composition while preserving readable subject cues.
- At whole-page scale and intended viewing size, the left pieces maintain photographic coverage across interior joins. White rectangular holes do not interrupt the body. Exterior paper and intentional silhouette steps remain legible without imposing the sculpture's contour.
- The right-facing body remains legible against the accent field. Its localized rectangular cut or darker middle segment is visible as intended. Overall continuity does not prohibit that interruption.
- Supplied secondary copy and footer occupy the lower-left hierarchy. Unsupported text remains absent. The accent bounds, paper border, crop, and open space support the agreed image-to-type balance.
- Actual anatomy and the overall composition take precedence over arbitrary sketch contours or numeric mask coverage. Material detail and exact text matter, but cannot rescue a lost subject or broken visual relationship.
- At both viewing scales, the paper, photograph, headline, and cobalt field read as one aged printed sheet. Subtle paper tooth and restrained ink-edge wear remain perceptible without obscuring the words, photographic fur, or right dark segment. A generic crack or dirt overlay does not satisfy the confirmed finish.
- Compare the result with both the intended adaptation and actual submitted prompt or production decisions. Attribute a failure to understanding, observation, design, prompt expression, or execution only as the evidence supports.
