# Compose and review a graphic design

Use this procedure for posters, covers, editorial layouts, infographics, and composed product advertisements. It applies to original work and reference transfer. The design method owns the process; visual analysis and other methods supply relevant evidence and specialist decisions.

First understand the communication task and the subject. Then observe the selected style reference. Then design the adaptation. Each stage produces decisions used by the next. Choose production tools only after those decisions explain the proposed image. A precise layout or a successful tool call cannot supply missing understanding.

Keep the reasoning in compact internal notes or the task's existing record. Do not require a new ledger, questionnaire, approval round, or fixed number of alternatives. A prompt-only task ends with complete usable prompts for the chosen path. A finished-artifact task also requires production and visual inspection. Pure photography, isolated 3D rendering, character turnarounds, tutorials, and existing image-and-prompt card rendering retain their own procedures.

This is Oneirloom's local synthesis. The [source notes](graphic-design-sources.md) identify external principles and their limits. They do not establish an industry-standard stage count.

## 1. Understand

Identify what the design must communicate, what the user has fixed, and what remains open. Carry the purpose, primary message, known audience and viewing context, deliverable, format, and exact supplied copy. Infer reasonable visual choices without inventing business facts, supporting claims, dates, or text. Ask only when missing information blocks the requested outcome.

Inspect supplied content and identity references at this stage. Understanding a subject requires seeing its form, silhouette, parts and their connections, defining colors and materials, and visible action. Determine which features make it recognizable and which relationships carry the intended meaning. For a character, consider what it does and how its parts support that action. For a supplied product, use product art direction for product facts and concept decisions. Do not choose a style treatment from a name or color alone.

Keep visible facts, meanings stated by the user, tentative readings, and unknowns separate. A crescent suspended in blue strands is visible evidence; sleep, care, or dream-weaving may be a useful reading, but remains an interpretation unless the user confirms it. A meaningful prop cannot be treated as disposable merely because it complicates a crop. Inspecting an identity image here does not replace the next stage's observation of the style reference.

Produce a short account of what the design must mean, what the subject is and does, which identity and semantic relationships matter, and what is still uncertain. This account must be concrete enough to judge later transformations. If it only lists a title, palette, and output size, understanding is incomplete.

## 2. Observe

Inspect the actual selected style or layout reference before using its stored recipe text. Assign each input its role, including identity, layout, style, or a result to critique. The recipe can help interpret observations, but cannot override visible evidence or a user correction. With no style reference, use the current content and communication context without inventing source observations.

Read the large composition first: frame, reading order, image mass, crop, visual axes, typography hierarchy, accent boundaries, and open space. Then inspect local construction. Count depicted versions separately from distinct identities. Describe each version's independent position, direction, visible body portions, and treatment. Trace how type and imagery interleave, where edges meet, and which layers appear in front.

Record regional tone changes and rectangular interruptions separately from displaced fragmentation. A globally continuous view can contain a local darker rectangle or cut. A central juxtaposition does not prove that one image continues across it. Inspect the whole image and its local relations before reducing it to a familiar technique.

At fragment joins, distinguish exposed background, touching or overlapping image pieces, and imagery carried by a lower layer. Separate interior gaps from exterior silhouette steps. Read the material finish separately as paper or other base, image medium, and ink-edge or printing character. Record how those parts relate across the page. A small source may support an overall finish without revealing microscopic texture or the original production process.

Carry only the relations that define this reference. Relative areas, alignments, borders, and approximate ranges can help describe them. Label estimates as estimates. A small source supports broad composition but may not establish exact cuts, font settings, coordinates, or hidden production steps. Keep visible facts, estimates, interpretations, unknowns, and user-confirmed corrections distinct.

Produce a source-grounded description of how the composition directs attention and how its image and text parts relate. Include the local details that change that reading. Use visual analysis for evidence discipline and applicable portrait, garment, or pose checks. A style-only reference does not require its subject, pose, or incidental copy in the adaptation.

## 3. Design

Bridge the understood subject and message to the observed visual language. Explain what each major transformation contributes. Select the reference relations to retain, the presentation choices to adapt, and the sample content to replace. For original work, derive those choices from the message and content. Explicit user intent takes precedence over a template default.

Resolve the visible subject portions, direction cues, fragmentation, crop, image-to-type scale, overlaps and layer order, secondary copy placement, accent roles, and any local tonal interruption that defines the design. For opposed views, decide how each side reads independently and how they work together. Use the new subject's own anatomy to establish direction. Moving an emblem to the left or right of an arbitrary shape does not by itself make that shape a convincing view.

Choose what displaced pieces uncover from the observed source and confirmed intent. Preserve image coverage where joins require it; retain separated slices or transparent gaps where those define the design. Resolve the page material, image medium, and final print or digital finish together. A defining material finish belongs in the adaptation, not an optional polish step. Clean digital work need not acquire aged paper or distressed ink.

Preserve recognizable identity and meaningful relationships through the chosen transformation. Cropping, desaturation, or fragmentation can be appropriate; full visibility is not always required. If an action-bearing feature is omitted or reinterpreted, resolve what happens to its meaning. For example, removing a character's hammock changes a suspended-rest relationship even when its star emblem remains. This is a decision for that subject, not a universal rule that all accessories must remain visible.

Translate compositional relationships instead of forcing the source subject's anatomical contour onto the new one. For example, a marble neck shape is not a creature body template. A rough sketch can explore mass and hierarchy, but its arbitrary contour is provisional. Real anatomy may require a revised sketch and artistic review. Never warp or crop the subject merely to satisfy a placeholder mask.

Resolve exact copy within the composition. A changed title length or line count can change the image-to-type relationship; design that change deliberately. Interwoven type requires a clear overlap order and readable subject cues. Omit unsupported small copy and rebalance the hierarchy. Do not automatically move the title to an unrelated empty corner or use absent copy as a reason to fill space with decoration.

Before production, check internally whether the adaptation explains why its major choices serve the message and reference relations. Could it retain every named object yet lose the intended action? Could its parts satisfy coordinates while the whole image loses identity or hierarchy? Return to understanding or observation when those questions reveal a gap. Prompt padding, model blame, or fixed pixels cannot resolve an unmade design decision.

Produce a resolved adaptation that connects intent, evidence, and visual choices, with reasons for material departures. This can remain concise internal prose. There is no mandatory user approval step or prescribed sketch count.

## 4. Choose production and execute

Choose a production path from the resolved design, requested deliverable, available tools, text-accuracy needs, and observed limitations. Direct image generation, native design tools, and compositing are valid conditional approaches. None proves design competence. Honor explicit requests for code or vector rendering, a particular model, or single-model production.

Use a sketch when it can resolve a material layout uncertainty. If you render one, inspect it as a provisional composition. Do not freeze its geometry before the subject and text relationships work. For a composite, use suitable existing assets and editable type where they help. Generate separate assets only when that serves the chosen path. Inspect actual asset shape, identity, direction, edges, and transparency before placement. Confirm cutout or alpha support from the actual entry.

For direct generation, write the complete intended composition, including subject, text, spatial relationships, and material. For a composite, supply the complete necessary asset prompts and the layout decisions needed to assemble them. Keep model controls and reference inputs outside copyable prompt text. Use complete Chinese and semantically equivalent English prompts unless the user requests another language scope.

State the resolved image coverage at joins and the relationship between page material, image medium, and final finish in the prompt or assembly instructions. Use observable appearance instead of a generic texture label or an unsupported claim about printing hardware.

Preserve successful content during execution. When actual anatomy or asset coverage contradicts a sketch, reconsider the composition instead of forcing the asset into its mask. Missing tools block only dependent work. A written plan, a generated asset, and an assembled poster are different outcomes.

## 5. Review the image

Review intended meaning and subject identity first, then the whole image-and-text language, before local polish or numerical compliance. Does the subject still convey the intended action or character? Do the visible versions and directions make sense? Does the title participate in the composition with the intended hierarchy and overlap? Are accents, local interruptions, and open areas doing their intended work?

For reference transfer, compare the actual source and result at the same visual height while preserving aspect ratios. Judge the agreed adaptation, including deliberate departures. Inspect occupied contours, crop, type groups, accent bounds, seams, fragmentation, local tone changes, and readable identity together. Then check exact copy, product facts, quantities, materials, and edges at the intended viewing size. A matched bounding box or mask-coverage score cannot establish visual success.

At whole-page scale and intended viewing size, check what each join reveals and whether image coverage or deliberate gaps match the design. Check the shared paper and ink appearance, or the chosen digital finish, alongside text legibility and subject detail. A texture overlay alone does not establish the intended material relationship.

Compare the result with the actual submitted prompt or production decisions separately from reference fidelity. Use the diagnosis method to locate failures in understanding, observation, adaptation design, prompt expression, or execution only as far as evidence supports. Missing prompts or execution records leave those causes unknown. Correct asset generation does not prove a correct poster; precise assembly does not prove a sound design.

Record the decisive matches, misses, and remaining uncertainty in the same working notes. If local mechanisms match while the whole design fails, say so. Never let local compliance substitute for the whole-image verdict.

## 6. Correct and deliver

Repair the responsible stage. An identity or meaning failure requires renewed understanding and design. A misread interruption requires renewed observation. With the design intact, fix a text or placement error in the available editable source, or use an appropriate image edit. Revise or regenerate only the content that needs it. Coordinate-only tweaks cannot fix an earlier conceptual mistake.

Continue authorized corrections without repeated approval requests. If the same critical miss survives two controlled corrections, use the diagnosis method's bounded investigation instead of adding synonymous instructions. Reuse the confirmed brief and successful work.

For a prompt-only request, return complete usable Chinese and English prompts for the selected production path. A direct-generation prompt describes the whole composition. A composite path includes its necessary asset prompts and assembly guidance. Do not force asset-only prompts or a layout plan onto every request. Keep analysis and review commentary outside copyable prompts.

For a finished graphic, deliver the final preview and state what was actually inspected and what remains unresolved. Include editable source and assets when the selected path or request warrants them. A prompt or completed queue without visual inspection is not a verified finished design.

Preserve historical prompts, images, and known metadata. Revised guidance does not inherit validation from older outputs, and one accepted example does not establish repeatability.

## Carry the decisions forward

Use compact internal notes or the existing task record. Carry the understood message and subject, evidence roles and uncertainty, observed reference relations, resolved adaptation and departures, selected production path, and actual review. Complete only what the task needs. Do not turn these notes into a schema or a separate ledger.

A template supplies concrete relations and sample choices within this process. Inspect its image, then adapt its guidance. Supporting palette, typography, and texture serve the composition. Creating a new subject or design does not require creating a persistent template.
