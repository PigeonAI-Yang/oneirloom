# Define and deliver a VI system

Use this guide to resolve the delivery scope already requested by the user. Select actual applications from the brand's work. A digital product may need an avatar, product header, social cover, and presentation; a physical shop may need signage, packaging, and a price list. These are examples, not mandatory bundles. Record the chosen scope in the existing task notes.

## Resolve the foundations

Keep a named inventory of the actual assets and their uses. Include the primary mark or wordmark, relevant secondary lockups, and needed monochrome and reverse versions. Identify whether each is approved, proposed, or missing. Preserve an approved master rather than silently redrawing it. If a compact symbol is needed, design and test that extension within the user's scope.

Derive clear space from a recognizable feature or useful dimension of the real mark. Establish minimum sizes through inspection in the target applications, with separate digital and print evidence where needed. Record background choices and any small-size version. An unrelated brand's measurements are not standards for this one.

Assign colors to roles such as signature, background, text, and limited accent. Record digital RGB or HEX values and the color space used. Inspect intended foreground and background pairs for their actual text and interface roles. Label an untested palette as proposed. For print, record verified process or spot-color specifications only when the printing need is known. Profiles, stock, ink process, and proof conditions affect appearance. A guessed RGB-to-CMYK conversion or nearest Pantone name is not a verified match.

Specify Chinese and Latin typography where the brand uses both. Resolve display, heading, body, caption, and numeric roles as needed; compare their apparent weight, height, line spacing, and punctuation. Separate custom wordmark shapes from fonts for ordinary text. State actual font availability and what is known about the intended license. Provide usable fallbacks when necessary. Font installation, purchases, or redistribution require their own authorization; a public type specimen does not establish permission.

Define auxiliary graphics through a repeatable rule: the source shape, how it repeats or crops, and where it supports content. Set icon and illustration style only to the extent needed by the applications. Define photographic subject, crop, light, and treatment when photography is used. Carry page material, image medium, and final finish together when they define the identity. Keep clean digital applications clean when that is the design.

Derive layout grids, scale relationships, and spacing from the real content and formats. Describe what stays consistent and what changes with aspect ratio, language, or content length. An arbitrary coordinate sheet does not substitute for these decisions.

## Prove the rules in real applications

Choose applications with different demands within the agreed scope, such as a small avatar and a dense information page. Use exact supplied content or label sample copy. Do not invent addresses, product claims, testimonials, or campaign facts to fill a mockup.

Create the actual artwork for each selected application. A perspective mockup demonstrates context; retain the flat artwork or editable source that a user can use. Inspect real-size legibility, safe placement, cropping, and the family relationship. Revise the shared rule when it causes repeated failure rather than patching each page into a different identity.

For character-led brands, preserve the approved character's meaningful parts and connections. Simplification can change detail for scale while retaining recognition and meaning. Distinguish the small mark from a detailed character illustration. Do not force the character into a generic mascot body or source logo silhouette.

## Assemble the manual and actual files

Write the guideline book from the resolved design. Cover the mark inventory and usage, color roles, typography, graphic and image rules, application examples, and file usage. Include only relevant misuse examples. Mark provisional rules and unavailable formats where the reader will encounter them.

Provide the actual master assets in formats the tools produced and the task needs. A raster logo inside an SVG wrapper is still raster artwork. Describe whether vectors contain editable paths, outlined lettering, live text, or embedded images. Keep the editable source when available, and do not claim unavailable formats from a preview.

Export the selected application files and preview them. Inspect transparency, image bounds, readable lettering, dimensions, and linked assets where relevant. For print-ready delivery, verify the printer's required format, dimensions, bleed, safe areas, image resolution, color setup, and proof requirements. A digital mockup does not establish print readiness. Missing print information can remain explicitly pending while digital work completes.

Report the guideline document review, actual export inspection, production verification, and user acceptance separately. A supplied specification is complete as a specification when its requested decisions are resolved. A full VI is complete only when the agreed foundations, applications, manual, and usable files are present. Scope omissions and failed exports remain visible rather than disappearing behind a polished contact sheet.
