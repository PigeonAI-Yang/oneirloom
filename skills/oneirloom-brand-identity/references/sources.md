# VI source notes

Reviewed on 2026-10-02. These public primary sources inform Oneirloom's local method. They do not establish a universal VI checklist or grant rights to another brand's assets.

| Source | Principle used | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [MIT Brand Guide](https://brand.mit.edu/) | An identity connects marks, color, typography, and applications. The site labels presentation examples as conceptual designs. | Its institutional architecture and asset range are not mandatory for other brands. |
| [MIT logo](https://brand.mit.edu/logos-marks/mit-logo) | A mark has context-specific variants, clear space, and small-size use. | MIT's measurements and placement rules belong to its own mark; derive new rules from the actual identity. |
| [MIT color](https://brand.mit.edu/color) | Color has hierarchy and legibility roles; screen, process-print, and spot-color specifications differ. Paper affects the stated print choices. | Do not reuse MIT palette values or infer a verified conversion for a new palette. |
| [MIT typography](https://brand.mit.edu/typography) | Type roles and fallback choices support consistent communication. | Access to a brand's font resources does not establish availability or licensing for this project. |
| [Tim the Beaver](https://brand.mit.edu/logos-marks/tim-beaver) | A mascot has a defined role within a wider identity. | MIT's character and permitted use are specific to MIT; no character artwork is included here. |
| [MIT templates](https://brand.mit.edu/applying-brand/templates) | Reusable applications make identity rules usable in ordinary communications. | Gated downloads are not part of this collection; the method supplies original briefs. |
| [MIT photos and videos](https://brand.mit.edu/applying-brand/photography) | Image resources form part of brand application guidance. | This source is not evidence for an invented universal photographic look. |
| [IBM UI icon design](https://www.ibm.com/design/language/iconography/ui-icons/design/) | A coherent icon family needs shared construction and optical attention. | Use the icon method for task-specific decisions; IBM's grid does not become every brand's grid. |

The public MIT pages were readable. MIT's downloadable brand resources are community-restricted; no restricted ZIP, font, or brand asset was fetched or copied. No login was attempted. Public source availability does not authorize asset redistribution.

The wordmark, mascot, and editorial templates are researched starter recipes. No corresponding generated image is available, and no end-to-end VI production run has tested them. Their design relations are Oneirloom adaptations, not copies of MIT or IBM designs.
