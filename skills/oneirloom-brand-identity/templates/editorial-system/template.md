# Build an editorial identity system

## Select this recipe

Use when a brand publishes changing content across posters, social posts, websites, presentations, or printed pages. The system must remain recognizable as the topic, text length, image, and format change. A single striking poster is one application.

## Define the family

Connect a stable typographic hierarchy and layout rhythm to a distinctive image treatment, controlled accent roles, and reusable auxiliary graphics. Keep the mark and essential identification readable while the content varies. Derive the grid from the brand's content rather than imposing one fixed poster composition.

If references define a material appearance, resolve the paper or other base, image medium, and ink-edge or digital finish together. Carry that relationship into suitable applications. An editorial identity can also be clean and digital. Off-white paper, distressed ink, fragmented sculpture, and red accents are source-specific options, not requirements.

Define what may vary, such as the hero image, column span, or headline length, and what carries recognition, such as type contrast, caption treatment, image finish, and accent limits. Crops and transformations must preserve the current subject's identity and meaning. Do not force a new subject into an old silhouette or fill absent copy with random graphics.

## Adjust the content and components

Resolve the exact brand names and approved marks, real content types, publication languages, type availability, palette roles, image sources, graphic mechanism, and target formats. A content brand with unknown priorities can begin with a clearly labelled proposal for a poster, social cover, and article or newsletter layout. Use actual context to replace that set.

Include the mark variants and supporting foundations required by the [delivery guide](../../references/vi-deliverables.md). Preserve existing approved marks while expanding their application language. Create a reusable layout source where requested, with editable content rather than a flattened example masquerading as a template.

## Resolve this production brief

Replace bracketed fields with current content and design decisions. The brief covers a repeatable identity across applications.

```text
Create the agreed editorial visual identity for [exact brand names and known purpose]. Preserve [approved marks, assets, and fixed choices]. Connect the family through [specific typography hierarchy and spacing rhythm], [color roles and bounded accent behavior], [auxiliary graphic rule], and [resolved image treatment]. Use [paper or object base, image medium, and final finish] only where these belong to the intended applications.

Provide the required mark inventory [master and relevant variants], legible type roles for [actual languages], and the supporting color and graphic specifications. Apply the system to [actual applications and formats] using [exact supplied content or labelled sample content]. Adapt image crop, columns, title line breaks, and content density to each use while retaining [defining family relationships]. Preserve [subject identity and meaningful connections] through image transformations.

Create usable artwork and [requested reusable layout sources] for the selected applications. Assemble a guideline book showing what stays fixed, what varies with content and format, and how marks, type, color, images, material, and auxiliary graphics work together. Deliver [master formats], [editable applications and actual exports], and [manual format]. Identify unresolved image rights, unavailable fonts, provisional print choices, and any uninspected output.
```

If the requested outcome is one presentation board, turn the resolved appearance into a complete prompt showing the system across the selected examples. Label it a concept board, not a delivered full VI. Provide complete Chinese and equivalent English image prompts under the main contract when generation prompts are requested. Keep exact image references and entry controls outside the prompt.

## Inspect the result

Compare the family at whole-page scale, then at intended reading size. Check whether changed content still produces recognizable hierarchy and image treatment. Inspect exact copy, mark use, accents, crops, and meaningful subject connections. Review material finish across type and imagery together. Judge the source adaptation separately from prompt compliance, and verify editable layouts and actual exports independently of mockups.
