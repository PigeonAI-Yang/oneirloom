# Action gestures

## Selection

Use this recipe when the reaction depends on body posture, movement, or an interaction with a meaningful object. The subject may be full body or partly cropped, provided the visible action remains understandable.

## Defining visual relations

Build the pose around a readable line of action and the actual character's anatomy. Separate overlapping limbs enough to explain the gesture, while preserving their attachments. Show the contact that gives a prop its meaning: a grasp, a catch, a supported weight, or another relationship grounded in the brief.

Use supporting marks only when they clarify movement or emotion. Keep them subordinate to the action. Change weight, direction, reach, or prop interaction between reactions instead of changing labels on one repeated pose.

For a referenced dream creature with four tentacles and a dream-catching net, resolve which tentacles hold or move the net and what the net does in this response. Do not invent a caught object to complete the pose. Account for four tentacles even when a deliberate crop or occlusion hides some parts. Explain the visible attachments and which part passes behind another form; plausible overlap cannot excuse an extra or missing appendage. Do not import that creature, net, or action into unrelated requests.

The degree of crop, viewing angle, action intensity, prop size, medium, and edge treatment are adjustable. Full visibility and fixed gesture coordinates are not universal requirements.

## Resolve the current brief

In the design notes, resolve the preceding turn, relationship, sender purpose, expected next action, and a likely neighboring misreading. For example, a forward reach can offer, request, or hand over an object. Decide which direction, distance, contact, and body response support the current use.

Fill the identity anchors, anatomy, connected gesture, meaningful prop interaction if any, framing, and shared appearance. Specify known part counts and deliberate occlusions without inventing unknown hidden structure. Choose a view that explains the action using known anatomy. Resolve the background from the requested deliverable and actual entry. Translate chat decisions into these visible relationships rather than adding analysis or claimed comprehension to the prompt.

## Prompt scaffold

Use this scaffold for one reaction. Resolve all slots, omit an unsupported prop sentence, and deliver both prompt languages under the method's output contract.

```text
Create one standalone chat-reaction sticker of {character with stable form, anatomy, colors, and identity details}. Show {full or partial body, viewing angle, and body orientation}. The body {concrete action and permitted deformation}, while {the known limb or appendage count, visible positions, attachments, and deliberate occlusions}. Show {the meaningful prop, its position and angle, and the exact contact with the character}. Arrange {the gesture's silhouette and direction} with {the resolved crop and overlap order}. Leave {the resolved clearance} around {the extended gesture or prop}. Use {the common medium, palette, material, visual scale, and edge treatment} with {the resolved background treatment}. Place {any necessary supporting marks and their scale} beside {the specific movement they clarify}.
```

Remove the supporting-marks sentence when none are needed. Each pack item needs its own resolved action; retain shared identity and appearance sentences across the prompts.

## Check the result

Read the action from the silhouette before inspecting fine details, then place it after the intended preceding message. Compare the likely neighboring interpretation. Check part counts, connections, permitted deformation, prop contact, overlap order, and direction. Trace intentional occlusion without declaring hidden anatomy verified. Confirm that a crop has not turned a reach into a disconnected fragment. Inspect the same reaction at its actual chat and picker sizes and check requested alpha against light and dark backgrounds. Judge any caption together with the pose. AI inspection does not establish a recipient's interpretation.

This recipe fails when all items share the same pose, the prop merely floats nearby, or expressive deformation alters identity. Correct the action design before adding decoration or relabeling the item.
