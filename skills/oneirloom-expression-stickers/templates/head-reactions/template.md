# Head reactions

## Selection

Use this recipe when a face, head, or compact identity region can communicate the reaction at chat-thumbnail size. Choose action gestures when body posture or a large prop carries the meaning. A faceless mascot can use its recognizable front or upper body; this recipe does not authorize adding facial anatomy.

## Defining visual relations

Keep the identity cue and the expressive cue close enough to read as one compact shape. Let the selected face change, head tilt, appendage movement, or emblem position convey the reaction before optional decorative marks. Preserve the connection between any visible gesture and the body.

Crop secondary anatomy only when the character remains recognizable and the intended action survives. Retain enough of an attachment, supporting body region, or prop to explain a meaningful gesture. A floating detached feature does not substitute for a deliberate crop. Keep the outer contour clear at the intended chat and picker sizes, with room around the cue that changes between reactions.

Framing, orientation, expression strength, medium, palette, border treatment, and canvas are adjustable. No circular badge, fixed facial proportions, or portrait-card grid is required. Sample emotions are replaceable content.

## Resolve the current brief

In the design notes, resolve the preceding turn, relationship, purpose, expected reply, and a relevant neighboring misreading. Decide which visible cue separates them. A lowered compact head region might suggest fatigue, shyness, or apology; its name alone does not settle the reading.

Fill the identity anchors, existing expressive feature, permitted deformation, crop, appearance, and edge treatment from the current request and inspected references. Keep facial anatomy limited to what this character has. If a caption is central, combine with the caption-reactions recipe instead of squeezing words into an unsuitable crop. Resolve a solid, transparent, or other background from the deliverable and the actual entry. Keep chat reasoning outside the prompt and translate it into concrete pose choices.

## Prompt scaffold

Use this complete scaffold for one reaction. Resolve every slot, then deliver the complete Chinese and equivalent English prompts under the method's language contract.

```text
Create one standalone chat-reaction sticker of {character with its stable visible form, anatomy, colors, and identity details}. Show {the compact head, front, or upper-body region} in {view, orientation, and crop}. Give {the existing expressive feature} {its concrete tilt, shape, position, or tension}, with {the resolved deformation and its effect on the surrounding form}. Show {any supporting appendage gesture, its body attachment, and overlap order}. Keep {the recognition feature} beside {the main expressive cue} as one compact silhouette. Leave {the resolved open space} around {the feature that needs clearance}. Render {the selected medium, palette, material, surface detail, and edge treatment} with {the resolved background treatment}.
```

Remove the supporting-gesture sentence when the design needs none. Write each pack reaction as a complete prompt with the same identity and appearance anchors. Vary the actual cue rather than reusing an unchanged head with a different emotion word.

## Check the result

Check the visible cue and identity before revealing a separate intent label. Read the result in its intended chat context and compare the relevant neighboring meaning. Inspect the crop for severed-looking appendages, lost emblems, or missing action-bearing features. For faceless characters, confirm that the selected nonfacial cue remains clear and no new facial anatomy appeared. Review the actual chat-size and picker-size image, including edges and requested alpha on light and dark backgrounds. If the item has a caption, judge its whole message; wordless clarity is not a universal requirement. Recipient understanding remains unverified without observed feedback.

This recipe fails when the crop removes the feature that communicates the message, or when expressive deformation changes the character's identity. Return to framing or cue selection in that case.
