# Expression-sticker templates

Choose by the visible cue that supports the intended chat response. First resolve the preceding message, relationship, purpose, expected reply, and nearby meanings in the method's design notes. Inspect supplied references before reading a recipe. Use the method directly when no recipe fits, or combine recipes under one pack's shared identity rules.

## Generic composition recipes

These three recipes work with the requested character identity. They have no associated example images and do not establish an observed style.

| Template | Select when | Defining relations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Head reactions](head-reactions/template.md) | A face or compact identity region carries the reaction at thumbnail size | Recognition cue and expressive cue stay together within a compact crop |
| [Action gestures](action-gestures/template.md) | Posture, movement, or prop contact carries the message | A readable action silhouette preserves connected anatomy and the relationship to a meaningful prop |
| [Caption reactions](caption-reactions/template.md) | An exact phrase carries or helps carry the response | Readable words form one visual unit with a compatible pose while preserving the requested character identity |

Each template records its own evidence state. These are three starter recipes, not a fixed reaction count, style, platform format, or verified output collection.

## Use an adaptation with your own identity

1. Select the transferable method: reaction coverage, pose decomposition, caption reserves, a comparison sheet, or an editable export workspace. Treat the listed counts and working canvases as design choices, not platform requirements.
2. Inspect the supplied identity. Record its actual parts, connections, palette, meaningful props, and supported views. With no image, use the brief and label the result as an unobserved proposal.
3. Keep only the relevant conversational intents and layout relations from the case study. Replace DreamWeaver's four-arm net actions with connected actions that the supplied subject can perform. Do not add its star, crescent, tail, palette, or wordmark to another identity.
4. Write a complete new prompt using those resolved actions and the user's exact captions. The branded English prompts in the linked recipes are worked examples, not universal prompt defaults. Supply the prompt languages required by the main method.
5. Produce and inspect the requested files through the main method. Use an actual editable source when requested, then select the destination's current export rules. The historical Oneirloom evidence does not validate the new subject or output.

For example, the receipt-versus-agreement distinction in Chat coverage transfers to a user-supplied two-arm robot as a restrained acknowledgment versus a stronger forward gesture. Resolve its actual arm joints and hand contact from the supplied reference. It does not acquire four arms or a moon-bearing net.

## Use a selected recipe

Resolve each scaffold into a complete prompt with visible form, gesture, crop, material, and any exact lettering. Keep chat analysis, neighboring misreadings, and review instructions outside the copyable prompt. Use the method's conditional production and animation branches rather than treating a recipe as a grid-export or animation procedure. Judge the actual result at its intended chat and picker sizes; a resolved scaffold remains untested until rendered and inspected.

The stored resolved examples use English instructions and preserve exact caption literals. Follow the main method's language contract when delivering prompts to a user. A white-background design preview is opaque; request and inspect real alpha separately when required by the chosen output.
