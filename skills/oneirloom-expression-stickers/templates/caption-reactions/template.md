# Caption reactions

## Selection

Use this recipe when a short exact phrase carries or helps carry the response. The character can reinforce its meaning or tone without independently saying everything in the words. Preserve the user's caption literally. A localized variant is a separate deliberate text choice, not an automatic translation of the words inside the image.

## Defining visual relations

Make the words and character one visual unit. Place the phrase above, beside, within an appropriate object, or along the gesture according to the current composition. Let the pose reinforce the words while keeping the character recognizable. Resolve the reading order and any overlap so that the caption does not cover the decisive expression or contact point.

Keep the letter baseline, spacing, stroke weight, and contrast readable at the intended size. A sloped or curved baseline can suit a gesture when the phrase still reads. Let the text outline or backing support letters on the requested backgrounds without swallowing the character or becoming a generic label box.

Text placement, line breaks, lettering style, crop, medium, and visual hierarchy are adjustable. No fixed top caption or bottom banner is required. Avoid a set of unchanged character poses with interchangeable text, or a text-only set when the requested character supplies the identity.

## Resolve the current brief

In the design notes, resolve the preceding message, relationship, sender purpose, expected reply, and likely neighboring misreading. Receipt and agreement can use similar positive imagery, so decide whether a pose contradicts the exact receipt caption. Consider sincerity, teasing, or deference only where the current context makes the difference relevant.

Fill the exact caption, language, identity anchors, compatible action, reading order, type placement, and edge treatment. Resolve a longer phrase through readable line breaks or a revised composition. Do not silently shorten or rewrite supplied text to fit. Keep conversational reasoning outside copyable prompts; express its visual consequences through pose, type, and their relationship.

Choose direct rendering or editable lettering after the design and according to actual text accuracy needs. For an explicitly selected single-model workflow, keep that constraint and report any unresolved lettering defect. A text plan alone is not a rendered caption.

## Prompt scaffold

Resolve every slot for one sticker, then deliver complete Chinese and equivalent English prompts. Keep the same exact in-image caption literal in both languages.

```text
Create one standalone caption-reaction sticker of {character with stable visible form, anatomy, colors, and identity details}. Show {the concrete body pose, connected gesture, and any meaningful prop contact} in {the chosen framing and view}. Render the exact caption "{user-approved caption}" {its position and scale relative to the character and gesture}. Use {the selected lettering style, weight, baseline, spacing, and deliberate line breaks}, with {the resolved letter color, contrast, outline, or backing}. Arrange {the first-read element} before {the secondary element} through {their size, placement, and overlap}. Leave {the resolved clearance} around {the decisive identity cue, action, and letter edges}. Render {the shared medium, palette, and material} with {the shared outer-edge treatment and resolved background treatment}, joining caption and character into {the resolved overall sticker silhouette}.
```

For a selected composite path, turn the resolved character and action into a complete asset prompt. Keep the exact caption, placement, baseline, styling, and overlap decisions in the assembly guidance. Deliver both the necessary asset prompt languages and the usable assembly instructions.

## Check the result

Compare every rendered character, punctuation mark, and line break with the intended caption. Preserve that same caption literal in both prompt languages. Check legibility, clipping, and visual attachment to the pose at the actual chat and picker sizes. Inspect identity and response meaning separately from spelling, then judge the full item after the intended preceding message. Check whether the pose introduces a neighboring meaning that the words do not intend. A caption-hidden view may diagnose the image's contribution; it is not a requirement that the sticker communicate fully without text. Confirm that the outline or backing works against relevant light and dark backgrounds, and verify real alpha when requested. Without recipient feedback, human understanding remains untested.

This recipe fails when the phrase changes meaning, letters merge or disappear, the caption covers the action, or the character becomes generic decoration. Repair the responsible text or composition decision while preserving successful identity and pose details.
