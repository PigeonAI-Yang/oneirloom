# Oneirloom sticker identity

Oneirloom (织梦师) is the brand. DreamWeaver (织梦兽) is its mascot. The older asset card calls the same mascot 梦茧兽. These names do not identify different characters.

## Approved visual reference

This document records the approved identity and its historical inspection. The reference image and lockup are private local assets, excluded from the public package. For brand-specific production, use them only when available locally under the user's authorization, or use an authorized reference supplied for the task. Without that image, the descriptions below remain recorded identity facts, not a new visual inspection. For another subject, use its own anatomy and the [generic sticker method](../SKILL.md).

The approved image establishes a rounded periwinkle cocoon with tufts, two arched antennae ending in gold balls, and a central navy oval containing one gold four-point star. Exactly four small arms grip blue strands of a hanging net. One gold crescent rests in its lower bowl. The short curled tail is on the creature's own left, which appears on the viewer's right in the front view. The image uses dark outlines and contains slight tonal variation on a white ground. Its white ground is not evidence of export-ready transparency.

The side, back, and action cards described in the source asset card are review drafts. Their new anatomy has not received separate user acceptance. They do not replace the approved front reference or require a new six-view sheet before sticker work. A slight front-view tilt can retain known anatomy. A true side or back view needs additional design evidence.

This reference remains an identity example, not a generated reaction sample or alpha example. ## Fixed identity and editable acting

| Element | Identity constraint | Allowed expression change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cocoon and tufts | Rounded cocoon, recognizably tufted contour, no added neck or legs | Lean, compress, stretch, or change the apparent weight while retaining the body and its connected parts |
| Antennae | Two arched appendages, each with one gold terminal ball | Vary arch, height, and asymmetry to support the body action |
| Dream window | One central navy oval on the front | Follow body deformation and perspective without becoming an eye or mouth |
| Star | One gold four-point star inside the window | Change its position, tilt, and apparent emphasis while keeping it contained and singular |
| Arms | Exactly four small attached arms, each accounted for in the pose | Bend and extend at different heights. The default reference pose has four net grips. A purposeful action may release one or two hands when the remaining identified grips visibly support the net and crescent |
| Tail | One short curl on the creature's own left | Follow body motion without swapping anatomical sides or becoming a fifth arm |
| Dream net | Connected blue woven strands held by explicitly identified arm grips | Raise, lower, widen, gather, or tension the net asymmetrically while the remaining grips preserve its load-bearing bowl |
| Crescent | One gold crescent held in the net | Settle within the bowl as its slope changes; remain supported rather than floating outside it |

The dream-weaving purpose comes from the user's brand definition. The window and star are nonhuman identity features, not a concealed human face. Facial terms in external templates translate into body, antenna, star, and net cues. They never require adding eyes, eyebrows, cheeks, or a mouth.

### Four-arm action construction

For the supported front view, label the arms by their visible positions. These labels describe staging, not unseen joints. In templates that use upper-left, upper-right, lower-left, and lower-right, the upper pair corresponds to the outer grips and the lower pair to the inner grips in the default hold. All left and right labels are viewer-relative. Follow each arm's body connection when a pose changes its hand height.

| Arm | Concrete role in the default hold | Role when the reaction changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Viewer-left outer arm | Grips the left outer suspension strand | Sets the net's left edge height and outward reach |
| Viewer-left inner arm | Grips the inner left strand near the body | Controls the left inner fold and prevents the bowl from flattening |
| Viewer-right inner arm | Grips the inner right strand near the body | Controls the right inner fold and supports the crescent's resting area |
| Viewer-right outer arm | Grips the right outer suspension strand | Sets the net's right edge height and counterbalances the left edge |

An excited pose can raise both outer grips while the inner grips retain a deep bowl. A cautious pose can bring all four grips closer to the body. A questioning pose can raise one outer grip and lower the other, with both inner grips keeping the crescent secured in the lower part. These are original acting proposals. They do not claim tested audience interpretations.

For each pose, describe what all four arms do and where the crescent's weight settles. Name the strand held by each gripping hand. If one or two hands release, describe their connected gesture and the remaining grips that carry the net and crescent. Keep all four arms accounted for. A net can be uneven without becoming disconnected. Strong body tilt does not remove gravity or turn the crescent into a detached decorative mark. Cropping may hide part of a limb only when its connection and the remaining grip arrangement remain intelligible.

These values are brand targets. The reference raster can contain nearby colors from shading and antialiasing. The default sticker treatment is flat illustration with clear dark contours. A selected plush, clay, or other material treatment can change texture, lighting, and edge softness while retaining the anatomy, palette relationships, net function, and part counts. A material change is not a new character design.

## Brand signature and composition

The mascot communicates the reaction. The wordmark identifies the collection on covers, contact-sheet headers, and master-document headers. Individual reactions normally omit the wordmark so the action and exact caption remain readable. A watermark on every sticker is not a default requirement.

A cover can place the complete front mascot above the lockup, with a clear gap between the net and lettering. A compact picker icon prioritizes the mascot and simplifies empty space. Tiny text is optional and must not displace the star, antennae, or net. Where an icon cannot retain enough identity detail, inspect the reduced result before deciding its crop.

The Oneirloom case studies use this approved identity as their starting point and supply original reaction planning or authoring structure. Generic use transfers the method to the supplied subject without copying DreamWeaver anatomy or branding. Template quantities describe those collections.
