# Product art direction

基于实物产品图构思创意广告的方法技能：先检查产品，再选概念，后成稿。它指导产品专属判断，不是提示词脚手架或预设风格。

## When to use

Use this method when a user wants a creative advertisement or story scene based on an actual product image, before selecting its style or template.

## Workflow

1. Inspect the product first. Look at the supplied image before choosing a scene, style, or reference case. Inspect the visible input directly, or use `view_image` for a local file. Reuse observations from the same unchanged product image if it was already inspected; inspect any changed replacement or crop. Record only what can be seen or what the user states: product and package silhouette, proportions, visible materials and colors, item count, marks, and readable wording. Keep product identity separate from the photograph's staging. Camera angle, placement, lighting, background, and supporting props can change unless the user asks to preserve them.
   Do not invent ingredients, brand names, origin, production methods, promotions, or product claims. Keep known wording and visible product details accurate. If no usable product image is available, do not write image-specific details. Give generic guidance if that is useful, and request the image only when it is needed to complete the requested prompt.
2. Choose one concept from the item and intent. Start from the user's intended message and the product's observed shape, material, and use context. Before consulting a stored case, form one concise concept that connects a real product property to a visible scene relationship and the requested mood or message.
   Identify the visible features that can carry the idea: for example, repeated shapes, folds, layers, surface texture, transparency, or a recognizable use gesture. Decide which feature remains literal and which can support a change in scale, arrangement, silhouette, or medium. For a multi-item meal, inspect the parts and their relationships instead of treating the whole box as one locked photograph. State the specific contact or transformation that makes the chosen scene belong to this product. A decorative background alone does not establish artistic integration.
   Carry forward explicitly requested styles and fixed spatial relationships. The inspect-first step determines how to realize them for this product; it does not silently replace the user's chosen direction. Distinguish requested constraints from incidental details inherited from an example.
   Choose a visual mechanism that the item can support. Scene elements should clarify the product or the user's intended story; an unrelated substance or setting cannot make a connection meaningful just by touching the product. Use a material transformation only when the product or the user's direction supports it. Do not assume a liquid spill, river, town, origin landscape, or any other recurring motif. Possible devices are examples to think with, not a required menu.
   Keep one main concept and a clear product-first hierarchy. Make the product the most recognizable and visually prominent object. Keep connected staging on a coherent ground plane, perspective, and light unless a deliberate transition has a visible reason. A sharp surface edge or mixed-media boundary can work when its relationship to the product makes sense; neither is a default. Keep transformations local to the product or its point of contact when that is how the concept reads. Do not split the image into unrelated scenes and use an arbitrary bridge to join them.
   Only after inspecting the current product and choosing its concept, consult a relevant example if it can help. The [historical chocolate poster case](../oneirloom-style-design/templates/product-origin-world/template.md) records one accepted product-specific result. Borrow only a relationship that fits the current item; the case does not establish a reusable recipe.
   An earlier takeaway transfer attempt left the meal unchanged above a table, placed a separate illustrated street below it, and used an oversized sauce waterfall as the only connection; the user found it forced and felt the food itself lacked artistic treatment. The later [takeaway food town case](references/takeaway-food-town/case.md) records a user-accepted result for the inspected source: spring rolls become shop roofs, fries form steps, and the opened kraft box carries the same hand-drawn street plane while the food remains recognizable. This supports that specific product-to-scene relationship, not a default town concept for other foods or a claim of repeatability.
3. Integrate the prompt. Build the complete prompt around the product's identity, chosen staging, and the specific visible relationship that carries the concept. Preserve packaging geometry, branding, known text, and product quantity unless the user requests a change. Keep the product legible and prominent as the scene becomes imaginative. State how the materials, contact points, scale, perspective, and lighting relate. Include only supporting details that strengthen the same idea. Treat a requested template as a visual intention to adapt to the inspected item.

## Output and handoff

Use the router's requested language format and deliver the complete prompt in one pass; do not seek approval after each internal decision. If a missing factual detail would change the image, ask only for that detail. Otherwise leave it open or use a clearly visual association without presenting it as a product fact.

When a generated result is available, compare it with the source image for product and package identity, known text and quantity, first-glance product focus, material continuity, and whether the concept reads from the product. For food, also check that the image keeps the food appealing. Revise the full prompt around the observed miss, or hand the miss to `oneirloom-result-diagnosis`. One accepted result supports its recorded prompt and product pairing, not general transfer to other products.
