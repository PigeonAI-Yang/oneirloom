# G — Graphic / 平面

**Scope.** Communication through typography, hierarchy, and arrangement of graphic forms.

**Choose this family when** the relationship among text, image, and graphic elements carries the message.

**Boundary.** Graphic describes composition rather than medium. Preserve supplied copy and route the requested artifact through the design process.

**Method owner.** [Graphic design](../../../oneirloom-style-design/SKILL.md) owns typography, hierarchy, and composed graphic forms. Packaging graphics belong here; package facts and product presentation stay with [product art direction](../../../oneirloom-product-art-direction/SKILL.md).

**Recipe and case routes.**

- For a product-origin world, consult the [accepted chocolate case](../../../oneirloom-style-design/templates/product-origin-world/template.md) and its [evidence record](../../../oneirloom-style-design/templates/product-origin-world/example-01.json). The record shows one accepted pairing of a packaged chocolate bar, flowing chocolate, and a cacao setting. It is not a default composition.
- The [experimental editorial poster collection](../../../oneirloom-style-design/templates/experimental-editorial-posters/template.md) describes twelve image-and-type arrangements. Choose one when its subject, crop, and type relationship fits the message. Its [reference record](../../../oneirloom-style-design/templates/experimental-editorial-posters/reference.json) identifies a supplied, inspected style sheet. Each poster's historical examples retain separate evidence.

Use product or space routes only when the product-to-scene relation or spatial arrangement changes the graphic composition. Typography and image/type hierarchy remain with graphic design.
